How to mention cash gifts or a registry on a wedding invitation

By the SendLove team · October 30, 2026

Quick answer

Keep gift details off the front of the invitation and put them in a separate insert, a wedding website or the details section of a digital invitation. Use one short, optional line such as "If you'd like to give a gift, a contribution toward our new home would be warmly appreciated," and never name an amount.

Most guests want to know what to give, and most couples feel awkward saying it. The answer is a short line, in the right place, with the right tone. Here's how to get all three right.

Can you put registry or cash gift details on a wedding invitation?

Traditional etiquette says no. The classic rule is that a formal paper invitation is about the invitation only, and gift details go somewhere else: a separate insert, a wedding website, or word of mouth through parents and the wedding party. The thinking is that putting gifts on the invitation itself can read like an entry fee.

That rule made more sense when an invitation was one printed card. Digital invitations have changed it. When your invitation has a separate details section that guests tap into, gift information sits there the same way a map or a dress code does. It's not on the front, it's not the first thing they see, and it saves your guests from having to ask.

And they do want to know. In many families, especially where cash is the usual wedding gift, guests would much rather have a bank or transfer detail than wonder whether to bring an envelope. Being clear is a kindness, as long as the wording is gentle.

How do you politely ask for cash instead of gifts?

The trick is to explain what the money is for, or to frame it as a choice, rather than simply listing an account. Guests like knowing their gift is going toward something. Here are wording examples you can adapt.

Simple and warm

Your presence is the greatest gift. If you'd like to give something more, a contribution toward our new home would be warmly appreciated.

For a honeymoon fund

We're lucky to already have everything we need for our home. If you'd like to give a gift, a little something toward our honeymoon would mean the world to us.

For couples who've lived together a while

After years of sharing a kitchen, we have more mugs than we'll ever need. If you wish to celebrate with a gift, a contribution to our future would be lovely.

Short, for a details section

Gifts: your being there is enough. For those who have asked, our details are below.

That last one is quietly useful. "For those who have asked" makes it clear you're answering a question rather than making a request, which is often exactly the truth.

How do you word a gift registry on an invitation?

Registries are a little easier because they're already a list of choices. Keep it to one or two lines and a link.

For those who wish to give a gift, our registry can be found at the link below.
We've put together a small registry for anyone who'd like ideas. Please don't feel any obligation.

Avoid the words "required," "expected," or anything that sounds like a dress code for wallets. And skip apologizing at length; a short line reads more confident and more gracious than a paragraph.

What not to say

  • "No boxed gifts." It's common, but it reads like a rule. "We'd prefer a contribution to our honeymoon" says the same thing kindly.
  • A bank account on the front of the card. Keep financial details in a separate section or insert.
  • Specific amounts or suggested ranges. Never. It turns a gift into a bill.
  • Jokes that could be misread. A pun about "funding our love" can be charming to friends and baffling to a great-aunt. Know your audience.
  • Several different payment methods with long instructions. One or two clear options are plenty.

Is it rude to ask for money as a wedding gift?

Not in itself. Cash gifts are normal in many cultures; at a lot of Lebanese, Arab, Chinese, Indian and Italian weddings, for example, money is often the usual gift, and couples may have a box or a family member collecting envelopes at the reception. In those circles, leaving out the details can actually make things harder for guests.

Where it can feel rude is in the delivery: a demand, an amount, or details placed where they look like a condition of attending. Gentle, optional wording placed in a details section avoids all three.

If your two families have different expectations (one side expects envelopes, the other expects a registry), you can offer both. "For those who wish to give a gift, you'll find our registry and our transfer details below" covers everyone without singling anyone out.

What about guests abroad?

This is where digital details really help. Guests flying in from another country may not want to carry cash, and posting a gift internationally is slow and expensive. A bank transfer, a local money-transfer service, or a registry that ships to you solves that.

A few practical tips:

  1. Use the method your guests already use. If most of your family sends money with a particular local app or service, list that first.
  2. Make numbers easy to copy. A guest typing an account number by hand on a phone is a guest who might get one digit wrong.
  3. Include the name the account is under. Guests want to know the money is going to you, not to a stranger with a similar name.
  4. Keep it secure. Only share what someone needs to send a transfer. Don't include anything you'd normally keep private, like card numbers or passwords, and send invitations to guests directly rather than posting them publicly.

What should you say when guests ask you directly?

Some guests will skip the invitation entirely and just ask: "What would you like?" This is your easiest moment, because they've invited the answer. Be honest and brief.

Honestly, having you there is the main thing. But if you'd like to give something, we're saving for our first place, so anything toward that would be amazing.

It helps if you and your partner say the same thing. Agree on one sentence and share it with both sets of parents and anyone in the wedding party, since they'll be asked too, often more than you. A mother of the bride who knows the answer saves a dozen awkward calls.

If a guest clearly wants to give a physical gift, let them. A registry link, or simply saying "anything you choose will be lovely," keeps it gracious. The goal of gift wording is to make giving easier, not to steer every guest into the same option.

Should gift details go on save-the-dates?

No. A save-the-date has one job: the date and the place. Gifts come later, with the invitation or on your website. Putting them on the save-the-date can make it feel like you're asking before you've even invited.

How do you thank guests for cash gifts?

The same way you'd thank them for anything else, with one addition: mention what the money is going toward. "Thank you so much for your generous gift. It's going straight into our honeymoon fund, and we'll think of you when we're sitting by the sea" feels personal without naming an amount. Never mention the amount in a thank-you.

Frequently asked questions

Is it rude to put registry information on a wedding invitation?

On the front of a formal paper invitation, many guests still see it as too direct. In a separate insert, on a wedding website or in the details section of a digital invitation, it's widely accepted.

How do you politely ask for money for a wedding gift?

Explain what it's for and make it optional, for example: "If you'd like to give a gift, a contribution toward our honeymoon would mean the world to us." Never mention an amount.

Where do you put bank details for a wedding gift?

In a separate gift section of a digital invitation, on an insert card or on your wedding website, not on the front of the invitation or a save-the-date.

Should I put gift details on my save-the-date?

No. Save-the-dates are for the date and place only. Share gift details with the invitation or on your website.

How do you word a honeymoon fund on an invitation?

Try: "We're lucky to have everything we need for our home. If you'd like to give a gift, a little something toward our honeymoon would be lovely."

How do you thank someone for a cash wedding gift?

Thank them warmly and mention what the money is going toward, such as your honeymoon or new home, without naming the amount.
