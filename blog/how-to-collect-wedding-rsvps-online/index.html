How to collect wedding RSVPs online

By the SendLove team · October 30, 2026

Quick answer

The simplest way to collect wedding RSVPs online is to put the RSVP inside a digital invitation and give each household its own link with a set number of seats. Guests tap accept or decline, and every answer lands in one list you can filter, chase and export for your caterer.

Online RSVPs can save you weeks of follow-up, or create a different kind of mess if they're set up badly. Here's how to choose a method, what to ask, and how to get every reply in on time.

What's the easiest way to collect wedding RSVPs online?

The easiest way is to put the RSVP inside the invitation itself. The guest opens the invitation, taps yes or no, says how many people are coming, and their answer lands in a list you can check from your phone. No separate form to find, no reply card to post back, no "did you get my text?"

There are other ways, and some of them are perfectly fine for smaller weddings. But the further the RSVP sits from the invitation, the more guests forget to answer. Every extra step (open another site, find your name, type a code) loses a few people, and those are the people you end up calling the week before the wedding.

What are the options for online RSVPs?

If you're only inviting close family, the last option is honestly fine. Past that, a system that collects answers for you saves real hours.

What should an online RSVP form ask?

Less than you think. Every extra question is one more reason to close the tab and "do it later." The essentials:

  1. Attending or not. A clear yes or no.
  2. How many people are coming, capped at the seats you've reserved for them.
  3. Names of the people attending, if you're doing place cards.

Then add only what you'll actually use:

  • Meal choice, if you're serving a plated dinner with options.
  • Dietary needs or allergies, as a short free-text field.
  • Which events they're coming to, if you're hosting a henna night, a ceremony and a reception on different days.
  • A message to the couple. Optional, but guests write lovely things here, and it's nice to read them later.

Skip questions like "song request" unless your DJ asked for them. They're fun, but they slow people down.

How do you stop people adding extra guests?

This is the question that keeps couples up at night, and the answer is simple: give each household its own link with a fixed number of seats. When the Khourys open their invitation, it says it's for two, and the RSVP won't let them pick five.

A single public form can't do that. Anyone can type "+3" and you're left deciding whether to send an awkward message. With personal links, the limit is built into the invitation, so the conversation never has to happen.

It also helps to be clear in the wording. Addressing the invitation to "Rami and Lara" rather than "the Haddad family" tells guests exactly who's included. If children aren't invited, say it kindly somewhere in the details rather than hoping people work it out.

When should the RSVP deadline be?

A common rule of thumb is three to four weeks before the wedding. Work backwards from your venue and caterer: ask them when they need a final headcount, then set your reply-by date a week or two before that, so you have time to chase the people who haven't answered.

A rough timeline that works for many weddings:

WhenWhat to do
6 to 8 weeks before (earlier for destination weddings)Send invitations with the reply-by date clearly shown
2 weeks before the deadlineCheck your list and send a friendly reminder to anyone who hasn't replied
Reply-by dateClose or freeze the list
The next few daysCall or message the last few non-responders personally
Caterer's deadlineSend the final headcount and any dietary notes

Put the deadline as an actual date ("Please reply by May 1"), not "within three weeks." People don't count.

How do you remind guests who haven't replied?

Here's where online RSVPs really earn their keep. Instead of trying to remember who said yes in a voice note, you open your list, filter to "no answer yet," and you have your reminder list in seconds.

Keep reminders short and warm. Something like:

Hi Maya! Just a little nudge, we're finalizing numbers this week. Could you let us know if you can make it? Your invitation link is below, it only takes a second ❤️

Send the same personal link again so they don't have to scroll back up to find it. For older relatives, a phone call is often quicker and kinder than a second message. One reminder is normal; by the second, just call.

Do older guests manage online RSVPs?

Mostly, yes, if the process is simple. The trouble usually isn't age, it's friction: a website that asks them to search for their name, a form with ten fields, a code they can't find. A big "accept" button inside an invitation they opened from WhatsApp is about as simple as it gets.

Still, have a backup. Tell older relatives they can just call you, and add their answer to the list yourself. If your RSVP tool lets you add a reply yourself, do that; if not, keep a short note and add it to your final list. Should you still include a paper RSVP card?

Only if you're already printing invitations for some guests. A paper reply card means stamps, a return address and a stack of envelopes to open, and the answers still have to be typed into your list by someone. That someone is usually you.

If you're mixing paper and digital, a neat approach is to print a small line on the paper invitation that says something like "Kindly reply by May 1 at the link below, or call us." Keep the digital list as the master copy, and add phone and paper replies to it the day they arrive. That way you never have two lists that disagree, which is exactly how a table ends up two chairs short.

For guests who will only ever reply by phone, that's fine too. The goal isn't to force everyone online. It's to make sure every answer, however it arrives, ends up in one place.

What about guests who speak different languages?

Mixed-language weddings are common, and the RSVP is the part where clarity matters most. If a guest isn't sure whether they've accepted or declined, you'll get wrong numbers. If your guests read different languages, choose an invitation that can show each guest the invitation, including the RSVP buttons, in their own language. For Arabic, check that it reads properly right-to-left rather than just translating the words.

How do you keep your RSVP list organized?

A few habits make the final weeks much calmer:

  • Build the guest list by household before you send anything, with a seat count for each.
  • Use one source of truth. If replies come in by phone, add them to the same list, not a separate note.
  • Export a copy to a spreadsheet near the deadline. Your planner, caterer and seating chart will all want it.
  • Track "no answer" as its own group, not as a maybe. It's the group that needs action.
  • Split the chasing. You take your side, your partner takes theirs. It's faster and less awkward.

Frequently asked questions

How do I collect RSVPs for my wedding online?

Use a digital invitation with RSVP built in, a wedding website with an RSVP page, or a simple form. Built-in RSVPs with a personal link per household usually get the most replies with the least follow-up.

What should a wedding RSVP form ask?

Whether they're attending, how many people are coming and their names. Add meal choice, dietary needs or which events they'll attend only if you'll use the answers.

When should wedding RSVPs be due?

Usually three to four weeks before the wedding. Check when your caterer needs a final headcount and set the reply-by date a week or two earlier.

How do I stop guests from adding extra people to their RSVP?

Give each household a personal link with a fixed number of seats, and address the invitation to the people who are invited by name.

What do I do about guests who don't RSVP?

Send one short, friendly reminder with their link about two weeks before the deadline, then call anyone still missing after the reply-by date.

Can I export my wedding RSVP list?

Many online RSVP tools let you download replies as a spreadsheet. SendLove's wedding invitations, for example, export the guest list to Excel.
