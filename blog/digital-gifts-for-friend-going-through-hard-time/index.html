Digital gifts for a friend going through a hard time: what to send, by situation

By the SendLove team · October 31, 2026

Quick answer

The best digital gift for a struggling friend is one that asks nothing of them: a short letter, digital flowers, a photo album of good memories, or a practical credit like a meal delivery. Match it to what they're going through and add "no need to reply."

Grief, a breakup, illness, a lost job or a quiet low patch each call for something different. Here's how to choose, what to write, and the mistakes to avoid.

When a friend is having a rough time and you're not close enough to drop off soup, it's easy to freeze. You don't want to send something that feels like a coupon, and you definitely don't want to send something cheerful at the wrong moment. The good news is that a digital gift, chosen for the actual situation, can land surprisingly well. The trick is matching what you send to what they're going through, and keeping the effort on your side, not theirs.

This guide is built as a decision guide. Start with the table, find the situation closest to your friend's, then use the sections below for what to send, what to write, and what to skip.

What's the best digital gift for a friend who's struggling?

The best one is the gift that asks nothing of them. When someone is grieving, sick, heartbroken or burnt out, their energy is already spent. A gift that needs a reply, a decision, a booking or a long read can quietly turn into another task on their list.

So the strongest options tend to share three things:

  • They open in one tap. A link they can view from bed beats an app they have to download.
  • They're about the person, not the problem. Photos of good days, a letter about why they matter, flowers with a short note. Nothing that summarizes their pain back to them.
  • They come with "no need to reply." Say it out loud. It removes the guilt of leaving you on read.

Practical gifts, like a food delivery credit or a streaming subscription, sit alongside these and can be the most useful thing you send. The best combination is often one practical thing plus one personal thing.

What do you send a friend who is grieving?

Grief is the situation where people most often get it wrong, usually by trying too hard to fix it. You can't fix it. What you can do is let them know you see them and you're not going anywhere.

A letter is the most meaningful option here. Not a long one. Share a specific memory of the person who died if you knew them, or say plainly that you're thinking of your friend. If you didn't know the person, it's fine to say so: "I never got to meet your dad, but I know how much you loved him from every story you told me."

Digital flowers are the softer, lighter option. They're a gesture rather than a conversation, which is exactly right for the first few days, when your friend is probably getting dozens of messages. Pair them with one line, not a paragraph.

Then there's the practical side. A meal delivery credit is the digital version of the casserole on the doorstep, and grieving people often forget to eat. If you go this route, say "I sent something for dinner this week" so they don't have to figure out what the email is.

One thing worth knowing: the hardest stretch is often after the funeral, when the messages stop. Put a reminder in your calendar for a few weeks later and for the first birthday or holiday without that person. A small gift then often means more than one in week one.

What should you send a friend after a breakup?

After a breakup, people lose a whole set of plans and routines along with the person. What helps most is a reminder that their life is still full of people who choose them.

A photo album of your friendship does this well. Fill it with trips, bad haircuts, the night you got lost driving home. Keep the captions light and specific. It shouldn't be a sad album; it should be a "look how much fun we have" album.

The other strong option is a plan. Not "we should hang out sometime" but an actual date for a video call or a visit, with a countdown so they can watch it get closer. Breakups make the next few weeks feel empty, and a fixed thing on the calendar helps.

Skip anything with romantic styling. A lot of digital gift sites are designed around couples, so check the design before you send. Hearts and "I love you" animations are the last thing a heartbroken friend wants to see from you.

What's a good digital gift for someone who's sick or in hospital?

Hospital stays and long recoveries are mostly boredom and discomfort, with a lot of waiting in between. The most useful gifts fill time without needing much focus.

  • Audiobook or streaming credit. Easier than reading when you're tired or on medication.
  • A group video. Ask five or six friends to record a short clip each and stitch them together. It's the closest thing to a room full of visitors.
  • Digital flowers. Some hospital wards don't allow real flowers, so a digital bouquet sidesteps the rules entirely.
  • A playlist you made for them. Free, personal, and good for long nights.

Keep your message focused on them as a person, not as a patient. "The group chat is unbearably boring without you" is better than "Hope the treatment goes well!"

How do you support a friend who lost their job?

Losing a job hits identity as much as income. Your friend might feel embarrassed, even if they'd never say it. The gift that helps most is one that reminds them what they're good at.

Write them a letter that's specific. Not "you're so talented" but "you're the person everyone calls when something breaks, and you fix it without making anyone feel stupid." Specific praise sticks in a way general praise doesn't.

If you want to add something practical, a grocery or meal delivery credit is useful without being pointed. Avoid anything that looks like you're managing their job search. Unless they ask for help with their CV or contacts, leave that alone.

What if your friend is depressed or just quietly not okay?

Here's the thing about low mood: one big gesture doesn't fix much, but steady small contact does. The best "gift" might be a standing commitment, like a call every Sunday, with a small digital extra now and then.

When you do send something, make it short and impossible to misread. A brief letter that says "I'm not going anywhere, and you don't need to reply" is enough. A photo album of good memories can help too, though check the timing; some people find it comforting, others find it hard to look at when they're low.

Avoid sending self-help books, positivity quotes or wellness apps unless they've asked. Those can feel like a hint that they're doing it wrong.

And if your friend says anything that makes you worried for their safety, a gift isn't the right response. Reach out to them directly, and encourage them to contact a local crisis line or emergency services.

What should you write with the gift?

Most people overthink the message and end up writing either too much or nothing at all. Short and honest works best. Here are lines you can borrow and adjust:

For grief

  • "I don't have the right words, so I'm just going to say I love you and I'm here."
  • "I keep thinking about the way your mom laughed at her own jokes. Sending you something small today. No need to reply."

For a breakup

  • "Made you something. Proof that you're excellent company and always have been."
  • "Calendar's booked. You, me, a video call on Friday, and absolutely zero talk about him unless you want it."

For illness

  • "Since I can't bring you terrible hospital coffee, here are some flowers that won't get confiscated."
  • "Everyone recorded a little something. Watch it whenever you've got the energy, or never. We just miss you."

For job loss or burnout

  • "Their loss. Seriously. Here's a list of things you're great at, in case you forgot."
  • "Dinner's on me this week. Don't argue."

For when you don't know what's wrong

  • "You've seemed a bit quiet. No pressure to talk about it. Just wanted you to know I'm thinking about you."
  • "Sending this for no reason other than you're one of my favourite people."

What are the most common mistakes?

  1. Sending something that needs a reply. Fix: add "no need to reply" to every message.
  2. Making it about how you feel. "I've been so upset since I heard" puts them in the position of comforting you. Keep the focus on them.
  3. Going too cheerful too soon. Jokes are great for some breakups and terrible for most bereavements. When in doubt, go gentle first.
  4. Giving advice disguised as a gift. A book about resilience can read as "you're not handling this well." Only send it if they asked.
  5. Sending once and disappearing. The follow-up two or three weeks later usually matters more than the first message.
  6. Picking a gift with the wrong tone. Preview it first. If it's styled for couples and your friend just got dumped, choose something else.

When should you send it?

Sooner is usually better than perfect. A simple message today beats a beautiful gift next month. That said, a few moments are worth marking in your calendar:

  • The first few days: something light, like flowers and one line.
  • Two to four weeks later: when the messages from everyone else slow down. This is where a letter or album lands best.
  • Hard dates: anniversaries of a loss, the first holiday after a breakup, the day of a big medical appointment.
  • A random Tuesday: no occasion, just "thinking of you."

Frequently asked questions

What do you send a friend going through a hard time when you live far away?

Send something that takes no effort to open or answer: a short letter, digital flowers, a photo album of your friendship, or a meal delivery credit. Add a note that says they don't need to reply.

Is a digital gift too impersonal for a friend who's grieving?

Not if it's personal. A letter with a specific memory or a few words about why they matter can mean more than a generic physical gift. Follow up a few weeks later too.

What should I write to a friend who is struggling?

Keep it short and honest, focus on them rather than your own feelings, and remove pressure with a line like "no need to reply, just thinking of you."

What should I avoid sending a friend after a breakup?

Anything romantic-looking, anything that mentions their ex, and advice they didn't ask for. A friendship photo album or a fixed plan to talk works better.

Are gift cards a bad gift for someone having a hard time?

No. A food delivery, audiobook or streaming credit can be the most useful thing you send. Pair it with a personal note so it doesn't feel transactional.
